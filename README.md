# Crewser

One Claude Code plugin for running several sessions side by side as a crew.

- **The crew** shows usage bars under the prompt in every session (context, 5-hour and weekly limits). Nothing else happens until a session starts or joins a task with `/crew-task`. At that point you name it (or it becomes e.g. `Implementer #1`). Every task gets a short code of its own, four digits (`#4821`), and the session's title is the task, its code and the name: `Checkout flow #4821 · Implementer #1`, so sessions of several tasks are told apart in Terminal's windows and tabs and in Claude's session list. The title is set through the prompt hook's `sessionTitle`, from the next prompt on; and its color, one no other member has, is applied with `/color` (if Claude Code refuses a plugin's run of it, the command is put in your prompt box: press Enter). It can see and ping only the other members of its task, and records the features it starts with `mcp__crewser__feature`. Several sessions then collaborate on the task, each with a role.
- **The town** (`/crew-town`) draws your task as a pixel-art town, made to read at a glance. The castle, named after the lead's project, stands with a few mountain massifs spread over the north, woods and meadows between them. Beside it is the **Tokens mine**, a building of its own: a rocky hill with a tunnel, a winding tower whose wheel turns, a cart bringing tokens out on rails, and a bin of gold tokens heaped as high as there is 5-hour limit left (empty when it runs out; the sign says how much is left). In front of the castle is the town square with a little spring and a few trees, which never changes. Around them lies open land: a lake to fish in with a pier, a river from the mountains, woods and meadows. A new project starts with almost nothing on it: the castle, the square, the mine, a footpath down to the lake and a short one to the mine. Every feature the crew starts is built as the kind of thing that fits it: a small house or a larger one, a shop, a tavern, a town hall, a school, a library, a workshop, a watchtower, a statue, a garden, an observatory, a blacksmith, a sawmill, a warehouse, a windmill, a farm and its fields, a dock, a harbor or a lighthouse. Claude picks the kind when it records the feature (`mcp__crewser__feature` with `kind`); a feature recorded without one gets a kind guessed from its name. A big feature made of parts is a farm (or a harbor) and its parts are fields of wheat, corn, cabbage, pumpkins, carrots, sunflowers, grapes or flowers (or docks), set beside it with `parent`. Each kind is built where it belongs (houses and shops round the square, farms and windmills out in the farmland, docks and harbors on the lake shore, the sawmill by the woods, the observatory up by the mountains), and a path is laid from its door to the nearest road when it is built, so paths grow with the town. Once placed, a building keeps its spot in its file and never moves. When teammates' towns are merged (a `git pull`, a merged branch), each building merges on its own: if two people changed the same building, git's conflict in that file is read as both sides and merged (every level either finished counts, the newest status wins, a burnt-down building stays burnt), and the building goes up again on its spot with confetti and a **merged** sign. If two new buildings landed on the same spot, the one started first keeps it and the other moves to the nearest free land; the parts of a big feature (fields of a farm) follow it. Everything has a dark outline, so people, buildings and props stand apart from the grass and from each other. Everyone in a crew dresses alike, in its lead's color, so who belongs with whom reads at a glance (their names keep their own session's color); a hat says their role: a crew's lead wears a dark captain's hat with a white plume and a cape, and has a ★ before its name, advisors and reviewers a wizard's hat, workers a hard hat. Their pose and place say what they do, with a sign over their head while they work: hammering at what they are building while they edit (`✎ editing cart.ts`), reading (at the library, if the town has one) while they read or search, at the anvil (of the blacksmith, if there is one) while they run commands (`▶ npm test`), pointing their helpers on while subagents run, thinking by the castle gate. Subagents are small helpers in their session's color carrying crates to the building site or scrolls. Things go up in stages you can see, with their name, the stage and a progress bar under them, and confetti when they are done. The town belongs to the project and stays between tasks, kept in the project itself (`.crewser/town/`, one small file per building, so it is committed and shared with the code; the **Town folder** setting puts it elsewhere on your machine instead): a later task sees what earlier crews built, and a feature started under an existing name improves it, a level up when it is done (★ per level). When the crew drops a feature (`mcp__crewser__feature` with status `remove`), it burns down: everyone gathers, sad ("Bye bye, Wishlist..."), the ruins smoke, then they cheer ("Well, time to start fresh, right?") and the land is free again. When the whole crew idles and you've been away a while, they line up and wave for work; idle members chat round the spring, nap under a tree by the lake or fish off the pier. Anyone out of sight gets a marker at the edge of the view (`◀ Tester #1`). `/crew-town` opens focused, with everyone already where their work is (hammering at their building, at the anvil, in the library, by the castle gate, or fishing and napping when idle), not walking there; someone who joins later walks in. Big framed buttons on the map's edges move the camera (▲ ▼ ◀ ▶, one on each side), the **−** and **+** in the bottom-left corner zoom, and **⌂ Castle** top right goes back to the middle; they take clicks in the fullscreen renderer. The same moves sit as buttons under the map, one per line, for the keyboard (Tab, or `h j k l` to move, `o` / `i` to zoom, `g` back to the castle); the wheel scrolls too, sideways over the top bar. There are two zooms, the overview (50%, where it opens) and the close-up (100%), each drawn with art of its own, so nothing is shrunk into a blur. Under the map is an order menu: pick a member (Tab + Enter, a click, or its number key), then **Give work…** (type the task inline), **Come up with an idea**, or **Cancel**. The member's own crew mod picks the order up within a couple of seconds and starts on it. The pane's own ✕ closes the town. Clicking needs Claude Code's fullscreen renderer (`/tui fullscreen`); the town offers a **Make it clickable** button for that. Every terminal cell holds two pixels, drawn with the lower half block so terminals that draw block characters a little short of the cell (macOS Terminal) still show solid pixels, in colors snapped to what terminals show, so it looks the same everywhere. Evenings dim the town a little and light its windows; people and their signs stay bright. `/crew-town demo` shows a demo crew, with a feature that is built and then dropped every ninety seconds.

Sessions share state through small JSON files in `~/.claude/crew/`. Being aware of each other costs no tokens. Only what sessions leave behind is cleaned up there (session records and orders after a day, ended tasks after a month); strategies and towns live in the project.

## Install

```
/plugin install crewser --marketplace MadsLangkilde/crewser
```

When it asks to add the marketplace, answer `y`, then pick the **user** scope so it loads in every project.

## Use

| | |
|---|---|
| bars under the prompt | `● Reviewer #1  ctx ███░ 58%  5h ██░ 31% ↻2h14  wk █░ 12%  ⚑ task #4821·role  👥 3` |
| `/crew` | the members of your task; pick one (its number, or Enter) and **Go to its window** (Terminal.app) or **Ping it** open under it |
| `/crew-add` | add to the running task's crew, also one started as a single session: one more of a role, or new roles in your own words, in tabs or windows |
| `/crew-stop` | stop the whole crew of your task now: after you confirm, every session of it quits and its Terminal windows close, this one too (also **■ Stop the whole crew** in `/crew-task` and `/crew`) |
| `/crew-task` | start a task (with ideas for what next), start another crew next to yours, resume one from before with its crew, join a running one, leave it, or decide what happens to a finished crew's branch |
| `/crew-overseer` | make this session the overseer of every crew in the project, above their leads, and talk to the leads from it; `/crew-overseer` again reopens the Overview, `/crew-overseer stop` ends it |
| `/crew-town`, `/crew-town demo` | the town view (a demo crew if nothing is running yet) |

A ping pulses the window until you look at it: focusing that Terminal.app window for about a second, or sending a prompt in that session, acknowledges it. In other terminals, where the window's focus can't be read, the banner has a **Seen** button.

Inside a task you can also just ask Claude, for example: *"which teammate is on the migration? I want eyes on it"*. Claude then uses `mcp__crewser__team` and `mcp__crewser__ping`. A session outside a task carries none of these tools.

### Several crews at once

Any number of crews can work on the same project at the same time, each on its own task or feature with a lead of its own.

- **Starting another crew**: run `/crew-task` → **▶ Start another crew** in a session that is already in a crew (or in the overseer's). It goes through the same three steps. The new crew gets a lead window of its own, which takes the task and opens its crew; your own crew carries on.
- **Isolation**: when another crew already works in the folder, the new crew gets its own git worktree beside the project (`../<project>-<task>`) on its own branch (`crew/<task>`). Its members work and commit there, so crews never edit each other's files or run each other's tests. Changes you had not committed in the main checkout are not in the new worktree. The first crew in a project works in the main checkout as before.
- **Finishing**: when a lead in a worktree ends its task, you decide what becomes of the branch: **Merge it into** the main checkout's branch, **Open a pull request** (pushes it and runs `gh pr create`), or **Keep the branch**. Merging commits what the crew left uncommitted first; if the merge conflicts, nothing changes and you are told. A crew whose lead window was simply closed waits for that decision: `/crew-task` → **⎇ Crew work waiting to be merged**, or the overseer's Overview.
- **Strategies and the town** stay shared: every worktree reads and writes them in the main checkout's `.crewser/`, so all crews build into one town.

### The overseer

`/crew-overseer` makes the session you run it in the overseer of the project: one session above the leads of all its crews, in none of them itself. Start it on purpose, in a window of its own; there is one per project.

- **What it sees**: the **Overview** pane (it redraws every couple of seconds) is in sections that read apart. At the top, a box with how many crews are running, how many members are working, the share of the 5-hour limit left (all crews draw from it), the cost, and how many finished crews wait for you. Then **⚑ RUNNING CREWS (2)**: a card per crew, framed in its crew's color, headed with its name and code (`⚑ Checkout flow #4821`) and its state on the right (**WORKING**, **IDLE**, **IDLE 12m** when everyone has been idle a while, **BLOCKED** when its last report says so, **PAUSED**). Under it: the lead, branch, how many are working, features done and cost; the goal; the last report to you; a line per member (★ for the lead) with its model, what it is doing and for how long (`working 3m · editing cart.ts · 2 helpers`, `idle 10m · last: run the e2e tests`), its context fill and **Go**; the features; and the crew's actions. **⚠ FILES MORE THAN ONE CREW IS CHANGING** follows when there are any (merges to plan). Finished work is apart from the running crews, under a green heading of its own, **⎇ FINISHED · WAITING FOR YOUR DECISION**: each crew, in a green frame, that stopped with its work on a branch, with **Merge it into the main branch**, **Open a pull request** and **Keep the branch as it is**. Last, **✉ CONVERSATION WITH THE CREWS**: what you sent and what came back, newest last.
- **Talking to the leads**: **✉ Message every lead…** at the top writes to the lead of every crew at once ("post me a one-line status"); each crew's **✉ Message the lead…** and **✉ Message the whole crew…** write to its lead or its whole crew. Or just tell the overseer in its prompt ("ask every lead how far they are", "tell the checkout lead to wait for search"). A message reaches its sessions within a couple of seconds as their next prompt, and they answer with a post that comes back into the overseer's session as a message (`[Checkout flow #4821 · Lead → overseer] …`) and into the Overview's conversation. Each crew card also has **Pause the crew** / **Resume the crew**, **Go to the lead's window** and **■ Stop the crew and close its windows…** (it asks on the card first); **+ Start a crew** is at the top.
- **What it does**: you tell it what you want across the crews ("search first, then the checkout; pause sound if tokens run low; start a crew on the README"), and it steers through the leads with tools of its own:
  - `crews`: the whole picture
  - `brief`: a message to a lead, a whole crew, or (crew `*`) every lead: instructions, a question, a request for a status. It arrives as their next prompt; their answers come back as messages
  - `retarget`: a crew's goal or priority
  - `pause` / `resume`
  - `stop_crew`: stop a crew at once, its sessions and windows closed (only when you ask for it)
  - `move`: a member's role to another crew; a new window takes the role up there, and the member hands over and leaves
  - `start_crew`: a new crew, in its own worktree when one is needed, its members in windows or tabs
- **Status and history**: every crew task keeps a history of what happened in it: started, resumed, ended, who joined or left, features started, finished or dropped, the overseer's messages, pauses, goal changes, and what became of its branch. Each session writes its own file per task (`~/.claude/crew/history/<task>/`), swept with the task after a month. Leads record where the task stands with `mcp__crewser__status` (when a feature is done, when blocked, when the plan changes, before the end); it goes into the history, to the overseer, and is what you read first when you resume the task. In the Overview every crew card shows its **Status**, and **☰ History: what changed and when** opens the crew's history in place of the overview: goal, status, features, then every change, newest first, by day, with who did it; **← Back to the overview** returns. Finished crews waiting for your decision have a History too. Leads answer what the overseer asks with `mcp__crewser__post` to `overseer`. Crews and members are named by their code across crews (`#4821`, `Implementer #1 #4821`), so two crews with the same roles never get mixed up. Merging stays your decision: the overseer tells you when a branch is waiting.

### The town with several crews

`/crew-town` shows every crew of the project at once. With more than one at work, each crew has a camp near the square: a tent and a pennant in its lead's color, with the crew's name over it. Its members gather in front of the tent when they are idle and go off to work from there. The overseer is the king: a gold crown and an ermine cape, at the castle gate, and the castle flies its color. Under the map, a **⚑ crews** row lists the crews (click one to move the camera to its camp), and the members are listed crew by crew for orders. The top bar counts the crews, who is at work and the tokens left. `/crew-town demo` shows two crews and an overseer.

### The town as its own window

`/crew-town window` (or **⧉ Open as window** under the map) opens the town in a separate window: a Chrome app window if Chrome is installed, else your default browser. It is drawn with real pixels at 60 fps. Drag to move, wheel to zoom, arrows or the on-screen pad to pan, click a hero for **Give work… / Come up with an idea / Go to its window**, or use the crew cards on the right. It needs Node.js; a small local server (127.0.0.1:47615) serves it from the crew files and stops by itself after half an hour unused. `/crew-town window demo` shows a demo crew.

### Strategies

A strategy is how a task is staffed: the roles (a lead, workers, advisors for different areas), how many sessions each role gets, what each does, which model and effort each runs on, and who advises whom.

- `/crew-task` opens the crew board on its first step: **Start a new task**, **Resume a task from before**, **Join a running task** or **Leave** yours (straight to starting when there is nothing else). Starting is three steps in the same pane: **1** what to work on, **2** the goal, **3** the strategy. Every step has **← Back** to the one before and **Cancel**, which drops the whole task setup.
- **What to work on**: a few ideas for the next task, from the project's recent commits, uncommitted changes, the town's features, earlier tasks and the README (a small model reads them as soon as you run `/crew-task`, and the step shows once they are in, all at once; the plain kinds of task if no answer comes within twelve seconds). Pick one (its number), and its goal comes along; or **✎ Type a name of your own…** (`t`).
- **Resume a task from before**: the project's tasks that ended, the last worked on first, each with its goal, crew, when it was last worked on and its status. Picking one shows, in a box, what it is about (a few sentences a small model writes from the task's history, kept with the task so it is written once per change), its goal, its status, the features done and in progress, and the last few changes; then its strategy and crew. Resuming makes this session the lead again with the same name, goal, strategy and town. **▶ Resume the task with the crew in tabs** or **▶ Resume the task with the crew in windows** brings every member back carrying on its own conversation: a tab or window opened again (`claude --resume`) for each that was closed, while a member whose window is still open rejoins by itself. **▶ Resume the task, just me** brings back the task alone. The lead keeps the crew in the task's file as they join, so this works however the task ended; for tasks from before this version the members are found by their transcripts.
- The strategy step (**Step 3 of 3**, on a line of its own, like every step) is headed **Pick a strategy**. On the left, under **Pick a strategy to use or modify:**, is the list: the strategies saved in the project, then the templates, then **+ Create a new strategy** and **– No strategy: just this session**. Move with ↑ ↓ (or click); the one in focus is drawn on the right with its duties.
- Picking one shows it drawn and asks **Do you want to edit this strategy before starting?** **No** starts the task with it. **Yes** lets you say what to change in your own words ("add a Fable advisor to the lead and two extra UI testers"): the team is redrawn with the change, and you choose **✓ Looks good** or **✎ Edit more**. Once it looks good: **Save updated strategy**, **Save as new strategy** (you name it), **Use it only this time**, or **← Go back**. **Change the roles by hand instead** opens the role editor.
- The board and `/crew-town` grow the Terminal.app window when it is too small to show them (never shrinking it, and keeping it on its screen). `/crew-town` also opens while Claude is busy with a turn.
- **Create a new strategy**: describe the team in a sentence and it is drawn for you, or start from one of the strategies or the templates and say what to change; it ends in the same **Looks good** and save choices.
- **By hand**: pick a role from the list, then pick its model (fable, opus, sonnet, haiku or the lead's), effort, number of sessions (**+ One more**, **− One fewer**) and whom it advises from the choices under it; its duty and name are typed in place; **+ Add a worker** and **+ Add an advisor** sit under the roles. The drawing updates as you go.
- Every choice in the board, the Overview, `/crew` and the town's panel sits on a line of its own, Back and Cancel too, so ↑ ↓ moves through them; nothing is side by side.
- The drawings always fit the pane: side by side when the crew fits, the boxes stacked in one column when it does not, a plain list on the narrowest panes. No line ever wraps.
- Starting with a strategy opens a Terminal window per session, each running Claude with its role's model and effort, joining the task by itself (`Checkout flow #4821 · Implementer #1`, `… · Implementer #2`, `… · Security advisor`), tiled on the lead's screen.
- **Windows or tabs**: chosen when the task starts: a strategy with more than one session ends in **▶ Start task with tabs** and **▶ Start task with windows**, one under the other (right in the strategy step, or after changing or saving the strategy); a task for this session alone just starts. Resuming asks the same. With tabs, every member opens as a tab in the lead's window, titled with its name, and the lead's own tab is selected again at the end. A crew started next to another one still gets a lead window of its own, and its members become tabs in that window: each crew is one window. `/crew-add` and the overseer's `move` open tabs too, in that crew's lead window. Terminal.app has no command for a new tab, so crewser brings the lead's window forward and presses ⌘T. That needs Terminal allowed under System Settings → Privacy & Security → Accessibility. crewser checks before anything opens: without it, the board says what to allow, with **⚙ Open the Accessibility settings**, **↻ I allowed it: … with tabs** and **▶ … with windows instead**, one per line. Tabs are not tiled, and `/crew-tile` leaves them alone.
- `/crew-strategy-manage`: the same list and drawing, to change or delete strategies; **Close** instead of Cancel.
- `/crew-strategy-new [description]`: describe the team in a sentence ("lead on sonnet, 2 opus implementers with an opus implementation advisor, a haiku tester"); you get a drawn draft to save, change or use. Without a description it opens **Create a new one**.
- `/crew-strategy-config [strategy]`: opens the board on changing a strategy: say what to change, or change its roles by hand.
- `/crew-strategy-use [strategy]`: start a task with one directly. `/crew-strategy-delete [strategy]`: delete one.
- **Stopping a crew at once**: `/crew-stop`, **■ Stop the whole crew and close its windows…** in `/crew-task` and `/crew`, or the same on a crew's card in the overseer's Overview. It asks first. Then the task ends (it shows under Resume, and a crew in a worktree has its branch waiting for your decision), every member's Claude quits, and the Terminal windows that hold only that crew close, including the one you pressed it in. A window that also holds tabs of other sessions stays open; only the crew's sessions in it stop.
- **Adding to a running crew** (`/crew-add`, **⊕ Add to this crew** in `/crew-task`, or **⊕ Add to the crew** in `/crew`): works the same whether the task has a strategy or was started as a single session. It shows who is at work now, then, one per line: **+ One more Implementer (sonnet)** for each role the crew has, **✎ Describe who to add** (in your own words: "a UI expert on opus who reviews the frontend", "two more testers"; the crew is redrawn with them), and **✎ Change the roles by hand**. The changed crew is drawn with who joins (`+ 1 × UI expert · opus · Reviews the frontend.`), and **▶ Add them in tabs** or **▶ Add them in windows** opens a session for each, with its model and effort, joining by itself. The change is kept as the task's strategy (the saved strategy it started from is not touched): every member's brief names the new roles, and a later resume brings them back. Fewer sessions close no one; members leave with `/crew-task` → Leave. You can also just ask Claude in the task ("add a UI expert in its own session"): it uses `mcp__crewser__add_members`. `/crew-tile` lays the crew's windows out again.
- Templates to start from: Duo, Feature team, Safe hands, Swarm. Saving a changed template saves it in the project in its place; deleting one hides it there. Strategies are kept in the project (`.crewser/strategies/`, one file each, committed with the code), so updating the plugin never touches them; the **Strategies folder** setting keeps them elsewhere on your machine instead. Strategies saved by versions before 0.13 (in `~/.claude/crew/strategies/`) still show up.

## Settings

`/config` (or `/plugin configure crewser@crewser`):

- which bars to show, and whether they sit under (default) or above the prompt
- bar style: blocks, shades, line, dots, squares or ascii
- bar width
- bar colors: traffic, session or mono
- whether the chosen name and color are applied with /rename and /color
- where the session color goes: `prompt` (`/color`), `background`, `both` or `none`
- tint strength
- whether the background flashes on a ping
- macOS notifications
- whether a joining session introduces itself automatically
- **Strategies folder** and **Town folder**: where strategies and the town's buildings are kept. Empty (the default) means inside the project, in `.crewser/`; a path (`~/crewser`, or relative to the project) puts them there instead, with a folder per project for towns.

Town settings: open at start, frames per second, height, and how long until an idle crew gets bored.

Window tinting, focus detection and jump-to-window use AppleScript in Terminal.app. iTerm2 gets tab colors. Other terminals get the bell and the in-app band.
